Stickers

Fifteen files, five designs. Each exists as a square or a circular die, and most in a dark and a light colourway. The checkerboard behind each preview is transparency, not part of the art.

The die is an instruction, not a line in the file. Every sticker here — round and square alike — is drawn as a filled 1.25″ square, because the background has to survive wherever the blade actually lands. None of them contains a drawn cut line, so the round ones look square in the previews below. That is correct, and it is also why the printer has to be told in words: 1″ circle, centred on the artboard, or 1″ square with a 0.125″ corner radius.

What actually differs between a square file and its round sibling is the layout: the round versions pull their content into a circular safe area, so nothing falls off the curve. Sending the square file to a circular die would clip it.

If a printer wants to see the cut and safe lines drawn on the art, the proof sheet at drafts/stickers.html shows every design with trim, die and safe area marked.

Smallest type on each design, measured rather than estimated. This is the clearance from the smallest lettering to the trim line, so a printer can tell you straight away whether their press holds it.

Dive square 3.53mm · Email square 2.59mm · Logo round 2.55mm · Republic square 2.20mm · Seal roomy 2.12mm · Dive round 2.05mm · Republic round 1.97mm · Email round 1.90mm · Seal 1.73mm.

A circular die holds materially less than a square one of the same width, because the available line narrows as you move away from the centre. That is why the round versions sit lower in that list, and why the roomy seal exists at all.

Certificate of citizenship

US Letter landscape. Spaces are left blank for the name, the date and two signatures — they are meant to be written and signed by hand.

Filecertificate-citizen.svg — Download
Size792 × 612pt, US Letter landscape, no bleed
Paper160gsm or heavier holds a signature without the pen bleeding. It prints on an office laser as-is — the border is inset far enough that a non-borderless machine will not clip it.

Citizen card

ID-1, the same size as a driving licence.

There is no press-ready card file yet. The card exists only as a browser preview, built by tools/build_idcard_draft.py, which lays it out in HTML rather than emitting artwork. Sending cards to a bureau needs a real 85.6 × 54mm vector file with bleed, plus a per-card data merge for the name, number, dates and QR code.

Colour

Hex values exactly as the files carry them.

Quay Navy#082A65
Basin Blue#0056B8
Water Blue#007FFF
Sign Red#FE010F
Lockup crimson#C13D4E

Sign Red will not survive CMYK. #FE010F sits outside the process-colour gamut — on a four-colour press it comes back noticeably duller and a little brown. For stickers, ask the printer for a spot red matched to the file. Do not let anyone convert it silently and send you a proof as if nothing happened.

The lockup is a different red. The supplied artwork is #C13D4E, a much softer crimson than Sign Red rather than a variation on it. Side by side, the two read as two different brands. The -signred version above exists so that the choice is a decision rather than an accident.

Known gaps

  • The card has no print file. Browser preview only, as above.
  • The Republic lockup is raster, not vector. It cannot be rebuilt from our own lettering: the traced sign alphabet is exactly eight glyphs — L, O, V, E, Q, U, A, Y — and REPUBLIC needs R, P, B, I and C, which the sign itself does not have. The source font or a vector file would fix this permanently and would unlock every other word too.
  • Sticker finish is undecided — matte or gloss, paper or vinyl, kiss-cut sheets or singles, and in what quantity.
