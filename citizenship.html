How it works

What forty dollars does not buy

Any right to the water that you did not already have. Nobody needs a card to swim at LoveQuay, nobody will ever be asked for one, and a citizen has no priority over anyone else on the deck. Article one of this Republic is that it is prohibited to prohibit swimming, and a paid card that bought special access would make a liar of it. You are paying for a card, an address, and the work — not for a place in the lake.

How it works

Subscribe, get a mailbox, get a card.

One

Subscribe

Forty dollars a year plus Canadian sales tax, billed annually by Thexyz. Payment and renewal are handled entirely on their side — nothing is collected on this site.

Subscribe at Thexyz

Two

Pick your address

Choose the name on your mailbox, subject to it being free. It is an ordinary mailbox: use it with any mail app you like.

Three

Get the card

A numbered card arrives with a code on the back. The number is tied to your subscription, not to your name.

Four

It stays good while you pay

The card reads as valid exactly as long as the subscription is active. Stop paying and it stops verifying — there is nothing else to cancel.

Check a card

The tax

Forty dollars, plus whatever Canada says.

Canadian sales tax applies, and it is not one number — it depends on where you are, not where we are.

In Ontario that is 13% HST, which makes it $45.20. In Alberta it is 5% GST, which makes it $42.00. Other provinces land in between. Thexyz works out the right figure at checkout, so the total you are shown there is the total.

Until the Republic is rather more autonomous than it currently is, we collect it like everybody else.

What the card shows

A number, and nothing else.

Anyone can check a card number, so the number deliberately gives nothing away.

Checking a card returns one of four answers: active, not active, no such card, or “could not reach the service”. It never returns a name, an email address or an account number, which means the checker cannot be turned into a way of harvesting @lovequay.com addresses.

The card number itself is random. It is not derived from your name, your email or the order you signed up in.

Know your quay

There is also a test.

Twenty-five open-book questions on the pier, swimming safely, cold water and looking after the place. Pass and the page gives you a thirty-two character citizenship code.

It costs nothing, it is not connected to the subscription, and it is not required for anything. It is a good way to find out whether you have actually read the safety page before you rely on having read it.

Take the citizenship test

The code is not a card

The test runs entirely in your browser and nothing about it reaches a server, so the code it produces is a receipt for having sat it — nothing checks it, and nothing is owed to whoever holds it. A card number is the other thing: issued, tied to a subscription, and answerable at the checker.

Plainly

This is a membership, not a nationality.

The LoveQuay Republic is not a state and claims no sovereignty over any water, land or person. A citizen card is not identification, carries no legal standing, and will not get you across a border or out of a parking ticket.

It is a card, an email address, and a way of paying for bin bags.
