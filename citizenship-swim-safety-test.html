The LoveQuay Republic

The citizenship test.

Know your quay. Know your limits. Keep it beautiful.

Twenty-five questions, about five to ten minutes. Twenty-two of them right and the page gives you a citizenship code.

Before you start

Open book, on purpose.

Becoming a LoveQuay citizen means more than enjoying Lake Ontario. It means understanding our waters, respecting the environment, recognising hazards and looking out for one another.

So go and read first. Nothing here is a trick, and nothing is timed. Everything the test asks about is written down somewhere on this site, and every question tells you afterwards where its answer came from.

Read the safety notes About the pier

How it works

  • 25 questions in four parts, four answers each.
  • 22 to pass — that is 88%.
  • Every question is marked at the end, right or wrong, with the answer and a link to the page it came from.
  • No limit on attempts. Take it again as many times as you like.

0 of 25 answered

The code

Thirty-two characters, and no magic.

Pass, and your device draws a code at random and shows it to you. It asks you for nothing first — no name, no email, no account — because there is nothing here to put them in.

It uses the same alphabet as a citizen card — thirty-one characters with I, L, O, U and Z left out, so nothing in it can be misread or mistyped into something else.

Write it down or screenshot it while it is on screen. Nothing stores it, so starting the test again gives you a different one and the old one is gone.

It is a receipt, not a credential

Nothing on a server knows the code exists, because nothing on a server saw you take the test. It proves you sat this page and got the answers right on the honour system, which is the only thing it has ever claimed to prove. The answers are in the page, and anyone determined enough can read them — that is what open-book means.

It is not a citizen card

A citizen card is a different thing: a number, an @lovequay.com address, and a subscription behind it that can be checked.

Plainly

Passing this does not make the water safe.

It is a quiz on a website. It is not training, not a qualification, and not permission. The pier is not owned, maintained or supervised by anyone, there is no lifeguard, and every person in the water is looking after themselves and the people they came with.

Never swim alone. Know your limit and stay within it.

A 400-metre quay running west out of Toronto's Inner Harbour into clean, deep, open water. Not owned, maintained, or supervised by anyone. Swim at your own risk, and keep it beautiful.
